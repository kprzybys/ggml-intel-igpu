## Reading
- The GPU cost of a batch is flat at ~140-150 ms up to k = 16 (Q4_K_M) and rises from k = 24: below that it reads the
  packed FP16 weights (~3.1 GB, ~115 ms at ~27 GB/s) plus waits; the compute-bound knee is k ~ 16-24 (the the reviewer /
  estimate k ~ 9-13 for skinny kernels was close; the packed GEMM runs here).
- The CPU batch path for k < 32 is not bandwidth-bound: Q4_K_M rises from 42 ms (k = 1) to 268 ms (k = 8), ~30-45 t/s
  flat. So for verification the GPU wins 1.9x at k = 8, 2.4x at k = 16, 2.6x at k = 24 (Q4_K_M); F16 from k = 8
  (1.1x), 2.1x at k = 16. The prediction "CPU wins verification up to 10-16 columns" (review_top5_fable.md) is
  falsified; mine (GPU from k ~ 4-8 on Q4_K_M) holds; "F16 from k = 2" is falsified (from k = 8).
- What it means for speculative decoding on Q4_K_M: plain decode on the CPU costs ~42 ms per token. One GPU
  verification of up to 16 drafted tokens costs ~145 ms = ~3.4 CPU tokens. It pays only if a step accepts more than
  ~3.4 tokens on average plus the draft cost. A draft model with per-token acceptance 0.7 gives ~3.3 tokens at k = 16
  (break-even, before the draft's own cost); cheap drafts (prompt lookup / n-gram) on repetitive output (code edits,
  quoting, summaries) can exceed it. [EST, acceptance rates not measured here]
- The real lever is the ~145 ms floor: it is the FP16 weight read, 3.2x the CPU's Q4 read. Verification on the GPU
  from Q4 weights directly (a skinny kernel reading Q4_K, k <= 16, where the decode cost does not matter because the
  kernel is memory-bound) would lower the floor toward ~40-50 ms and make speculation a clear win: this reverses the
  verdict of results/qgemm-model-2026-11-13.txt for the small-k case. [HYP, next to model]
